- **Mass-movement validation:** `MovementCase` and `MovementCapacity` remain
  diagnostic scaffolds until comparable episodes are coded over time. A cascade,
  durable organization, and successful political outcome must not be treated as
  the same variable. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/why-masses-act-together.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Representation validation:** `RepresentationCase` and `GatekeeperRisk` now
  cover the intermediary layer between participants and a state, company or
  closed group, but they remain diagnostic constructs. Need matched cases and
  time-ordered data to separate internal participation, external responsiveness,
  discipline, selection power and actual accountability. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/why-states-emerge.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/governance-field-guide.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Historical function switch:** `RepresentationEvolutionCase` separates an
  assembly’s origin, later resource role and eventual constraint on authority.
  Current evidence is strongest for selected medieval European cases; broader
  cross-regional comparison is still needed to distinguish coordination,
  compellence, bargaining, diffusion and geography. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/why-states-emerge.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **State-formation sequence:** `StateFormationCase` now separates origin,
  function switch and later consolidation across accounting technologies,
  colonial legibility, European fiscal reform and a postcolonial negative test.
  Need more matched cases with dated evidence on information carriers,
  intermediaries, representation, succession and persistence; the new succession
  layer adds historical tests, but current material
  does not justify a universal war, writing or bureaucracy theory. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/why-states-emerge.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/historical-regional-cases.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/state-formation-case-coding.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Succession and persistence:** `SuccessionPersistenceCase` now separates rule,
  uncertainty, elite bargaining, bureaucratic memory, capacity after change and
  voice after change. Evidence covers 28 European states over 800 years, 300+
  Eurasian dynasties and 293 medieval cities, but historical selection, working-
  paper status and long causal channels require matched cases before transfer to
  firms or closed groups. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/how-leaders-emerge-and-change.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/why-states-emerge.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/historical-regional-cases.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/state-formation-case-coding.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Capacity versus lived outcome:** `EverydayOutcomeCase` now separates fiscal
  input, authority chain, local knowledge, service access, quality, distribution,
  extraction burden, voice/appeal and persistence. China, Russia, India, Nepal,
  Portugal and Vietnam provide contrasting tests, but service-level and
  subnational evidence is still needed to distinguish public goods from
  extraction-linked infrastructure. (`docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/why-states-emerge.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/historical-regional-cases.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/state-formation-case-coding.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Culture and ritual validation:** `NormGovernance`, `RitualTradeoff` and
  `TieControl` are diagnostic constructs. Need matched cases that separate
  coordination gain from dissent cost, outgroup harm, participant burden and
  relationship dependence; strict practice alone is not evidence of abuse.
  (`docs/wiki/research/culture-religion-evidence.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/religion-ritual-and-group-coordination.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/closed-groups-and-new-religious-movements.md`)
- **Closed-group exit validation:** `NRMExitCase` now separates group durability,
  leader centralization, resource and relationship dependence, exit mode, actual
  loss, external support and identity recovery; `NRMExitTrajectory` adds ordered
  points before entry, during membership, at leader change or exit, and after exit.
  One small three-year NRM panel provides direct individual evidence; succession
  histories and a general-religion cohort provide bounded comparators. Most cases
  still lack independent data on material loss, retaliation, appeal, identity and
  long-term well-being, so the coding frame is not yet a validated causal index.
  (`docs/wiki/research/culture-religion-evidence.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/closed-groups-and-new-religious-movements.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Cross-domain transfer validation:** `CrossDomainCase` now aligns state, firm,
  movement/NGO and closed-group fields for public task, information, resource
  dependence, intermediary, authority, implementation, voice, appeal, exit,
  persistence and capture. Three new comparative sources support contingency,
  not a universal institutional form; matched time-ordered cases are still needed
  to test which mechanisms actually transfer. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/comparative-governance-cases.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Mass governance and control validation:** `MassControlCase` now separates
  state capacity tasks from political control: public task, service access and
  distribution, information quality, intermediary, co-optation, repression,
  participation, voice, appeal and exit. The new evidence connects surveillance
  to repression and public-goods trade-offs, informational autocracy to perceived
  competence, and post-conflict control modes to local ties. Need matched,
  time-ordered cases that test whether higher compliance reflects service capacity,
  consent, dependence or fear. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/how-mass-information-is-governed.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Participation and accountability validation:** `ParticipationCooperationCase`
  now separates rule origin, leader selection, participant scope, voice,
  accountability, sanctions, mobilization, elite capture, cooperation, service
  outcome and persistence. Experiments support conditional cooperation gains from
  democratic rule choice and visible elected leadership, while Liberia, Congo and
  the participatory-development review show task, context and durability limits.
  Need time-ordered comparisons that distinguish real influence from attendance,
  symbolic consultation and intermediary capture. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/why-masses-act-together.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Legitimacy and compliance validation:** `LegitimacyComplianceCase` now
  separates authority source, process fairness, consistency, accuracy, voice,
  sanction severity, trust, intrinsic and extrinsic compliance, correction,
  appeal and exit. Experiments and meta-analyses support conditional links among
  fairness, legitimacy, cooperation and organizational outcomes, but causal
  translation into real institutions and the post-sanction period remain open.
  Need matched time-ordered cases that distinguish perceived legitimacy from
  normative justice and from mere dependence or fear. (`docs/wiki/research/articles/power-authority-legitimacy.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Metric governance validation:** `MetricGovernanceCase` now separates true goal,
  proxy, observability, task bundle, incentive, target tightness, worker voice,
  gaming, information quality, quality, equity, safety, learning, correction,
  exit and persistence. Evidence shows both useful target effects and context-
  dependent nulls or distortions; need longitudinal matched tests of independent
  outcomes, hidden tasks, bad-news flow and post-target behaviour. (`docs/wiki/research/management-evidence.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Management durability:** `ManagementDurabilityCase` is still a diagnostic
  scaffold. Need repeated observations after intervention, supervisor turnover,
  practice adoption, worker voice, retention and independent outcome measures;
  short-run productivity gains do not prove institutional durability.
  (`docs/wiki/research/management-evidence.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/why-firms-use-hierarchy.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/how-leaders-emerge-and-change.md`)
- **Institutional learning and change:** `InstitutionalLearningCase` now separates
  signal independence, trial, comparator, decision gate, discretion, memory,
  transfer, leader turnover, voice, appeal, rollback, forgetting, capture and
  short/long outcomes. Evidence shows that policy feedback, pilots, networks and
  routines can support adaptation, but selection, temporary over-effort,
  misinterpretation, inertia and attention trade-offs can create false learning.
  Need matched longitudinal cases across state, firm, community and closed group
  with independent outcomes, ordinary-regime follow-up, succession tests and
  observed rollback or persistence.
  (`docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/management-evidence.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/comparative-governance-cases.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Matched trajectories:** `MatchedInstitutionalTrajectory` now gives one table
  for baseline, comparator, intervention, implementation lag, process/quality/
  distribution/resource outcomes, persistence, rollback and alternative
  explanations. Initial contrasts show useful state-capacity and management
  effects, partial decay, historical rule bursts and enforcement backfire. Need
  a smaller set of genuinely comparable cases with pre-registered coding,
  independent data, consistent time windows and observed leader or rule turnover;
  mixed designs must not be presented as one pooled causal estimate.
  (`docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/comparative-governance-cases.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Institutional decay, capture and reform:** `InstitutionalDecayCase` now
  separates gradual loss of capacity, elite capture, independent task failure and
  genuine recovery. The new cases show that government audits, civil-service
  autonomy and protected delegation can improve delivery, while expertise,
  centralization, retrenchment and bureaucratic memory can preserve capture or
  turn short-run improvement into long-run decline. Need matched cases with
  pre-shock baselines, independent distribution/resource outcomes, safe voice and
  exit, observed leader turnover, and a real rollback or persistence test.
  (`docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/management-evidence.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/comparative-governance-cases.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Matched decay audit:** the first three-domain audit now distinguishes a
  randomized state-capacity effect, partial organizational decay and a historical
  commons trajectory. It still cannot pool them: the commons lacks an equal
  comparator and full welfare outcomes, the firm lacks complete long-run profit,
  quality and worker-voice data, and the state case lacks leader-turnover and safe-
  exit tests. Next growth point is one instrument with the same time windows,
  distribution measures and rollback criteria in all three domains.
  (`docs/wiki/research/governance-of-groups.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
- **Safe reform and rollback:** `SafeReformCase` now requires a protected signal,
  independent review, stop authority, correction, compensation, exit, rollback,
  postmortem and persistence after personnel change. Robodebt is a documented
  negative case in which adverse review and complaints failed to stop a harmful
  automated process; policy-reversal studies show that rollback risk also changes
  over a policy's lifecycle. Need field data showing whether the protocol lowers
  correction latency without turning every signal into a veto or making exit more
  costly.
  (`docs/wiki/research/governance-field-guide.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/articles/measuring-voice-appeal-exit-feedback.md`;
  `docs/wiki/research/evidence-matrix.md`)
